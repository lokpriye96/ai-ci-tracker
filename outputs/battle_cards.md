# Competitor battle cards: GenAI in IT services

_As of 11 Nov 2026. 'How to compete' is written for a hypothetical mid-tier challenger. Strengths and gaps are analyst judgements based on the tracked public events ([data](../data/ci_events.csv))._


## Accenture

| | |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | 'Reinvention partner of choice': the most client-focused, AI-enabled firm |
| **Flagship AI assets** | Accenture Anthropic Business Group; OpenAI flagship programme; Faculty Frontier; ~110k AI & data staff |
| **Key moves** | Anthropic and OpenAI alliances (Dec-25); Faculty acquisition, whose CEO became CTO (Jan-26); 17 deals worth $4.9B in FY26; Sagacity and Avicado (Oct-26) |
| **Strengths** | Largest M&A firepower; C-suite access; allied with both leading model labs; pre-built industry assets (telecom, construction, customer) |
| **Gaps / watch-outs** | FY27 growth guidance of only 3-6% in local currency; premium pricing; many acquisitions to integrate at once |
| **How to compete** | Compete where price and outcome certainty matter (run-heavy deals, mid-market). Offer fixed-outcome pilots and faster time-to-value than a large Accenture programme |

## TCS

| | |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | Aims to be 'the world's largest AI-led technology services company' |
| **Flagship AI assets** | HyperVault AI infrastructure (100MW with OpenAI); Claude business unit; ADD AgentHub; Gemini Enterprise across its portfolio |
| **Key moves** | AMD (Jan); OpenAI and HyperVault (Feb); ServiceNow (Feb); Google Cloud (Apr); Mistral Forge (May); Anthropic Global Premier Partner (Jun); Best Buy GCC (Q2 FY27) |
| **Strengths** | Broadest bench of model partners; AI revenue of $3.1B, more than 10% of the total; huge delivery scale; strong BFSI franchise |
| **Gaps / watch-outs** | Headline growth is slow (~2.8% YoY in constant currency in Q2 FY27); India revenue dipped; so many partners risks diluting focus |
| **How to compete** | Present breadth as complexity. Lead with one deep, certified platform partnership and a named team the client can meet |

## Infosys

| | |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | AI-first services firm targeting a $300B+ AI services opportunity |
| **Flagship AI assets** | Infosys Topaz and Topaz Fabric; AI First Value Framework (6 value pools); Anthropic CoE (telecom); Columbia Enterprise AI Center |
| **Key moves** | Anthropic (Feb); Optimum Healthcare ($465M) and Stratus ($95M) (Mar); Google Cloud (Apr); Columbia centre, OpenAI Codex and Truist GCC (Oct) |
| **Strengths** | Clear AI service taxonomy that buyers can follow; new depth in healthcare and P&C insurance; Finacle as a ready channel for banking AI |
| **Gaps / watch-outs** | Disclosed AI revenue share (5.5%, Dec-25) is below the share TCS claims; two healthcare and insurance acquisitions still to integrate |
| **How to compete** | In healthcare and insurance, expect Optimum and Stratus references. Counter with outcome-priced pilots and platform-neutral delivery |

## Wipro

| | |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | Consulting-led, AI-powered firm with a separate AI-native platforms business |
| **Flagship AI assets** | Wipro Intelligence (WINGS, WEGA, NetOxygen, CROAMIS); LIFT framework; 1,500 forward-deployed engineers on Gemini |
| **Key moves** | HARMAN DTS ($375M, closed Dec-25); AI-Native Platforms Unit (Apr); Mindsprint with $1B+ Olam deal (Apr); Alpha Net contracts; ServiceNow (May); Google Cloud and LIFT (Aug) |
| **Strengths** | Creative deal structures (captive plus contract); IP separated from services; engineering depth from DTS |
| **Gaps / watch-outs** | No frontier-lab alliance tracked; shares down ~35% YTD by Aug-26; one low-confidence event (Aggne) still unverified |
| **How to compete** | Probe for model-neutral depth beyond Google. Lead with multi-model options and production references in the client's industry |

## HCLTech

| | |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | Services plus software: AI Force across engineering, operations and applications |
| **Flagship AI assets** | AI Force 2.0; AI Foundry; HCLSoftware (UnO Agentic, Actian, Jaspersoft); OpenAI Advanced Partner |
| **Key moves** | AI Force 2.0 (Apr); $1.14B AI deal, Sarvam $150M and Jaspersoft (Jul); ₹3,500 cr AI data centres (Jul); Guardian GCC (Jul); Robotiq.ai (Sep) |
| **Strengths** | Owns software IP and is now building its own AI infrastructure; won a $1B+ AI-led deal; sovereign-AI stake |
| **Gaps / watch-outs** | 'Advanced AI' revenue ($620M) is defined narrowly and differently from peers; several software acquisitions to integrate |
| **How to compete** | In infrastructure and run deals they bundle software. Position on openness and freedom from lock-in, and ask about exit costs |

## Cognizant

| | |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | 'AI Builder': bridging AI investment and enterprise value |
| **Flagship AI assets** | Neuro AI and Neuro AI Trust; Flowsource; OpenAI (25k) and Gemini (18k) Frontier Capability Groups; Astreya AI OpsHub |
| **Key moves** | 3Cloud (Jan); Microsoft (Jan); Cognition (Jan); Astreya ~$600M (Apr); Frontier talent pledge (Sep); Activate unit, Google and OpenAI groups (Oct) |
| **Strengths** | Most aggressive public talent commitments; a measured proof point (37% net saving with Cognition); stack running from AI infrastructure to software engineering |
| **Gaps / watch-outs** | Announcements lean on headcount and badges, with few named client results; four big acquisitions in 18 months |
| **How to compete** | Ask for production references, not certification numbers. Lead with measured case studies and accountable delivery leads |

## Capgemini

| | |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | Catalyst for enterprise-wide AI adoption, led by agentic 'Intelligent Operations' |
| **Flagship AI assets** | WNS Intelligent Operations; Google Cloud AI Enterprise Hub; Outcome Deployed Engineer pods |
| **Key moves** | WNS $3.3B (Jul-25, closed Oct-25); Cloud4C (Nov-25); GenAI over 10% of Q4 bookings (Feb); Google Hub (Apr); sale of US federal unit (Sep); Q3 +11.7% (Oct) |
| **Strengths** | Largest AI-motivated acquisition tracked; strong in operations and BPO; European sovereignty credentials |
| **Gaps / watch-outs** | About 5 points of 2026 growth is acquired, not organic; ~€700M restructuring; leaving the US federal market |
| **How to compete** | Capgemini plus WNS is strong in operations deals. Compete on transition risk, US-specific references and price |

## Tech Mahindra

| | |
|---|---|
| **Positioning** | Telecom-first AI and India-built ('sovereign') AI platforms |
| **Flagship AI assets** | Project Indus; TechM Orion; ontology-driven agentic platform with Microsoft; ServiceNow AI & Innovation CoE |
| **Key moves** | Microsoft agentic platform for telecom (Mar); Avant acquisition (May); ServiceNow Client Zero and CoE (Aug); CoRover BharatGPT (Sep) |
| **Strengths** | Deep telecom domain; sovereign-AI story; credibility from using the ServiceNow platform itself first ('Client Zero') |
| **Gaps / watch-outs** | Fewest AI events tracked (4); no frontier-lab alliance tracked; small-ticket M&A |
| **How to compete** | Outside telecom their AI story is thinner. Compete in BFSI and manufacturing with stronger model partnerships |
